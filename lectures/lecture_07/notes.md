## Slide 6 — The interest–growth snowball

The usual first-order approximation is Δd≈(R_debt−g_nominal)d_previous−pb. Keep the exact denominator when calculating. The effective interest rate on the debt stock differs from the yield on a newly issued ten-year bond, so a market move passes through as debt resets or is refinanced.

## Slide 8 — Growth above interest can permit a primary deficit

## Slide 10 — A rising ratio is a warning, not an instant default test

Stabilising the ratio is a useful diagnostic, not a full necessary-and-sufficient solvency condition. Country-specific institutional capacity, contingent liabilities and political choices matter. A single universal safe debt threshold is not justified. Distinguish gross debt from net debt and central from general government.

## Slide 11 — A long maturity delays the cash-flow effect

## Slide 12 — Fiscal fatigue and a possible adjustment limit

The feasible primary-balance response rises, flattens and eventually weakens in this illustrative political/economic capacity function. The crossing is a model-dependent limit, not a measured safe-debt number. Endogenous yields and growth can move both schedules.

## Slide 14 — Nominal repayment and real value are different promises

Avoid the claim that domestic-currency debt can never default. Institutional and political constraints can cause nominal default, and monetary financing can change inflation and credibility. A currency-union member lacks unilateral control over the common monetary issuer. Foreign-currency debt is a different promise again.

## Slide 15 — Fiscal dominance

Define fiscal dominance as a regime/mechanism, not a synonym for high debt or any bond purchase. Monetary dominance typically involves monetary policy pursuing price stability with fiscal adjustment supporting debt obligations. Real-world regimes require evidence about policy reactions, institutions, expectations and fiscal backing.

## Slide 17 — QE changes the public sector’s liability mix

The government bond asset held by the central bank and the Treasury liability cancel in a consolidated view, but reserve liabilities remain. If reserves pay a floating policy rate, purchases can shorten effective public-sector rate exposure. Currency pays no nominal interest and has different properties. QE can also affect duration premia and market functioning.

## Slide 33 — Why Japanese capital need not immediately return home

Higher JGB yields can make domestic assets more attractive at the margin, but repatriation is not an accounting inevitability. Compare matched risks, maturities, collateral, currency hedge tenors and costs. A lower foreign-home short-rate gap can reduce hedging cost while the domestic yield rises, creating competing effects.
