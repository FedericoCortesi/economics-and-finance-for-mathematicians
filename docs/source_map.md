Ten- and thirty-year yield charts show basis-point changes from each series’ own 1 July level. FX charts rebase each quote to 100 on 1 July. - **US:** [Treasury July daily par yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202607) and [August table](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202608), selected 10Y and 30Y columns.

- **EUR:** [ECB AAA sovereign par-curve observations](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/data/YC/B.U2.EUR.4F.G_N_A.SV_C_YM.PY_10Y+PY_30Y?startPeriod=2026-07-01&endPeriod=2026-08-31&format=csvdata). This is an AAA sample curve, not a single euro-area government bond or an average of all national borrowing costs.
- **FX:** [ECB reference rates, USD and JPY per EUR](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/data/EXR/D.USD+JPY.EUR.SP00.A?startPeriod=2026-07-01&endPeriod=2026-08-31&format=csvdata). JPY per USD is derived as (JPY per EUR)/(USD per EUR), using the same ECB reference observation.

The endpoint 10Y changes are US +27.0 bp, Japan +23.2 bp and EUR AAA +37.4 bp. The 30Y changes are +28.0, +20.9 and +31.1 bp respectively. These are descriptive changes from source-native series. Local closes/fixings and yield conventions differ, so the comparison is not a synchronous executable relative-value trade. JPY per USD falls about 1.83% while USD per EUR rises about 1.87%; JPY per EUR is almost unchanged between endpoints. Endpoint arithmetic does not describe every move inside the window.

The charts do not identify a causal repatriation story. Expected policy rates, inflation expectations, term/risk premia, supply, positioning, hedging costs and investor transactions are competing or complementary explanations. The exercise asks which additional evidence would discriminate among them. The observations were retrieved for this course on 15 October 2026 and are frozen in the supplied CSV.

## Lecture 1: US external financing and exorbitant privilege

The revised first deck uses a goods-market planned-expenditure plot at fixed prices, a Cobb–Douglas potential-output example, and an explicit percentage output gap before applying the accounting to US financing. The detailed change-in-NIIP reconciliation remains in the speaker notes.

- [IMF: What Is the Output Gap?](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2013/09/basics.htm) supports the discussion of slack and inflation pressure. The Cobb–Douglas function and numerical gap are modelling examples, not estimates of US potential output.
- [BEA: Q2 2026 international transactions and investment position](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-transactions-and-investment-position-2nd-quarter-2026), release 24 September 2026, supplies the quarterly current-account balance and end-quarter external stocks. Exact observations, units and the source URL are frozen in `../data/us_external_position_2026Q2.csv`. The current-account dollar flow is not annualised. The position includes the entire resident economy and equity as well as debt.
- [Federal Reserve: The International Role of the US Dollar, 2025 edition](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-international-role-of-the-u-s-dollar-2025-edition-20250718.html) explains international demand for dollar assets and their liquidity services. Its evidence is dated and does not establish the effect of subsequent 2026 events.
- [Gourinchas and Rey: From World Banker to World Venture Capitalist](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11563/w11563.pdf), NBER Working Paper 11563, supplies the classical research framework for external-portfolio returns and exorbitant privilege.

The income example (assets 100 earning 6%, liabilities 150 paying 2%) is an original hypothetical calculation. It explains how a negative NIIP can coexist with positive investment income. It is not a claim about the current US income balance. The market examples hold other inputs fixed and do not infer a dollar or yield forecast from the sign of NIIP. Capital gains, income flows and net positions remain separate concepts.

## Lecture 2: Fisher, money demand and post-Covid evidence

The opening distinguishes sticky-price short-run experiments from the flexible-price long-run benchmark, where the output gap closes. There is no fixed calendar cutoff: changes in real fundamentals can move potential output and the neutral real rate, while nominal shocks have different implications across horizons. The next slide connects bank credit, a bakery oven and household spending through an original invented illustration.

The rate notation is local to this deck: i is nominal, r is real, ī is the central bank’s chosen policy target, and r* is the neutral/full-employment benchmark. P is the price level; the ZCB price is lower-case p₀. The classic model solves for market i at a fixed monetary quantity, while the rate-target alternative closes the main lecture.

- **Textbook ch. 16, printed pp. 459–462 and 480–482:** the long-run Fisher effect and the distinction from sticky-price monetary experiments. The deck separately derives the exact realised purchasing-power conversion, its logarithmic/first-order approximation, and the forward-looking approximate real rate. An exact realised identity does not identify causality. Under inflation uncertainty, an expectation of a ratio cannot generally be replaced by a ratio using expected inflation.
- **Textbook ch. 19, printed pp. 647–648, exercise 13:** saving/investment supply and demand for the equilibrium real rate. The displayed numerical curves and shifts are original hypothetical examples of this full-employment model; they add behavioural assumptions to the ex post saving identity.
- **Textbook ch. 15, printed pp. 423–426:** real money demand, its interest opportunity cost and market clearing. The LM explanation follows portfolio adjustment via bond prices, with the relevant fixed-money assumptions stated.
- [BoJ, 21 September 2016](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2016/k160921a.pdf): historical yield curve control, including the ten-year JGB target around zero. It is an example of direct long-yield targeting, not a description of current Japanese policy.
- [BLS headline CPI via FRED, CPIAUCNS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS) and [Federal Reserve target upper bound, DFEDTARU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFEDTARU): the US post-Covid chart uses January 2020–September 2026, retrieved 15 October 2026. `../data/us_inflation_policy_2020_2026.csv` freezes the exact inputs and transformation. Inflation is 100 × (CPI/CPI twelve months earlier − 1), using the non-seasonally-adjusted index. The policy line uses month-end observations of the target upper bound. The missing October 2025 CPI is a visible gap, not an interpolated value.

The historical chart describes sequencing, not a causal estimate of the policy effect. Observed trailing CPI is not expected future inflation; subtracting it from a plotted policy rate does not produce an ex ante real rate. The fixed-spending supply-shortfall example and MV=PY page explain why higher prices do not mechanically require the money stock to rise. M/P is a purchasing-power stock used for transactions, not real GDP itself. The original Phillips relationship is retained. Old-to-new page locations and the response to each annotation are in `lecture_02_revision_map.md`.

### Compounding, instrument names and policy communication

The latest Lecture 3 annotations replace the simple-rate arithmetic-average presentation with exact forward compounding and an interval-specific forward-minus-expected-rate wedge. [Fed research](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2005/200533/200533pap.pdf) discusses forward premiums defined by this difference. The teaching notes distinguish a forward premium from a holding-period expected excess return and do not interchange expectations and products.

Before the matched-date curve, the deck defines a zero-coupon spot rate and distinguishes a traded [Treasury STRIP](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/strips/) from the [Fed fit to off-the-run coupon bonds](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/nominal-yield-curve.htm). The yield-sensitivity section fixes cash flows and remaining maturity while varying a yield selected from the term structure. DV01 is expanded as dollar value of one basis point, with a currency example; see [New York Fed usage](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1070.pdf).

The single official-data dot plot and its explanation are in Lecture 2's policy-feedback section. Both Lecture 3 newspaper prompts include visible clickable article links. The [annotation map](lecture_03_revision_map.md) gives current page locations.
